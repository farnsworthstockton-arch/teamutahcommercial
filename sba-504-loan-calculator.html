How SBA 504 Loans Work

The rules this calculator uses, in plain English, with SBA's own wording and links.

What a 504 loan is

An SBA 504 loan helps a business buy or build the building it will use, or buy long-life equipment, with a smaller down payment. SBA says:

“The 504 loan program provides long-term, fixed rate financing for major fixed assets that promote business growth and job creation.” “504 loans are available exclusively through Certified Development Companies (CDCs).”

A typical 504 project has three pieces:

  • A bank loan for 50% of the project cost, with the first (senior) lien on the property.
  • A CDC loan for up to 40%, with the second (junior) lien. A Certified Development Company (CDC) makes it, and it is funded by a bond SBA guarantees 100% (SBA calls the bond a “debenture”).
  • Your down payment of at least 10%. SBA says it “may be borrowed as long as it is subordinate to the Third Party Loan and the 504 debenture”, meaning any loan for it must rank behind both of the other loans.

“Typically, a project involving a 504 loan includes: A loan secured from a private sector lender with a senior lien covering up to 50% of the project cost A loan secured from a CDC — backed by a 100% SBA-guaranteed debenture — with a junior lien covering up to 40% of the total cost A contribution from the borrower of at least 10% equity of the total project cost”

Sources: SBA, 504 loans (page updated August 28, 2026, per its page data; read October 21, 2026); SBA, lender resources (page updated October 21, 2026, per its page data; read October 21, 2026); SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026; read October 21, 2026).

When the down payment is higher

The down payment rises to at least 15% for a new business (2 years old or less when the loan is approved) or for a limited or special-purpose property, and to at least 20% when both apply. The bank's share stays at 50% and the CDC's share shrinks.

How much SBA will lend

SBA's share (the CDC loan) can be up to $5,000,000 for most businesses, counting the 504 loans the business and its affiliates already owe, or up to $5,500,000 per project for small manufacturers and certain energy-saving projects. The bank loan and your down payment are on top of that, so total projects can be much larger.

“For all 504 Projects, the gross debenture is limited to an outstanding maximum balance of $5,000,000 in the aggregate for each small business concern, including its affiliates, except for Eligible Energy Public Policy Projects and Projects for Small Manufacturers”

Fees financed into the CDC loan count toward the limit, because SBA measures the “Gross Debenture”, which it defines as “the Net Debenture Proceeds plus the eligible administrative costs.” The smallest CDC loan is $25,000: “The minimum dollar amount for a debenture is $25,000.”

For small manufacturer and energy projects, this calculator uses the $5,500,000 per-project limit and does not subtract existing 504 loans. SBA's rules for combining those projects with a business's other 504 loans are more detailed than this calculator handles, so ask your CDC.

Source: SBA SOP 50 10 8.1, “Debenture Limits (Minimum and Maximum)” (effective October 1, 2026; read October 21, 2026).

Loan terms and interest rates

The CDC loan runs 10, 20 or 25 years: up to 25 years for real estate and at least 10 years for machinery and equipment. The bank loan must run at least 7 years with a 10-year CDC loan, and at least 10 years with a 20- or 25-year CDC loan.

“10-, 20- and 25-year maturity terms are available.”

The CDC loan has a fixed rate, set when SBA sells the bonds that fund it. That rate follows U.S. Treasury rates: usually the 10-year Treasury for 20- and 25-year loans, and the 5-year Treasury for 10-year loans. The bank sets its own rate. This calculator has no built-in rates, so type in the quotes you get.

“Pegged to an increment above the current market rate for 10-year U.S. Treasury issues.”

Sources: SBA, 504 loans (page updated August 28, 2026, per its page data; read October 21, 2026); SBA SOP 50 10 8.1, “Loan Maturities” and “Interest Rates” (effective October 1, 2026; read October 21, 2026).

Who can use a 504 loan, and for what

Your business has to occupy the building. 504 is for buildings your business will occupy: at least 51% of an existing building, or 60% of a new one. It can't be used to buy investment rental property.

“For an existing building, the Applicant must occupy 51% of the Rentable Property and may lease to a third party up to 49%; or For new construction, the Applicant must occupy 60% of the Rentable Property, may permanently lease to a third party up to 20% and temporarily lease an additional 20% with the intention of using some of the additional 20% within 3 years and all of it within 10 years.”

If your real-estate company owns the building and leases it to your business (SBA calls these an Eligible Passive Company and an Operating Company), SBA says: “The EPC must lease 100% of the Rentable Property to an eligible OC(s).”

To be eligible, SBA says your business must:

  • Be an operating business
  • Operate for profit
  • Be located in the U.S.
  • Be small under SBA size requirements
  • Not be a type of ineligible business

SBA adds: “Loans cannot be made to businesses engaged in nonprofit, passive, or speculative activities.”

A 504 loan cannot be used for:

  • Working capital or inventory
  • Consolidating, repaying or refinancing debt that does not meet the definition of “qualified debt” under 13 CFR 120.882, paragraphs (e) and (g)
  • Speculation or investment in rental real estate

Sources: SBA, 504 loans (page updated August 28, 2026, per its page data; read October 21, 2026); SBA SOP 50 10 8.1, “Occupancy and Leasing Requirements” (effective October 1, 2026; read October 21, 2026).
