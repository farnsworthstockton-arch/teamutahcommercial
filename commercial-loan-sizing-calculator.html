Year Balance at start Payments Interest Principal Balance at end
  • Payments = the monthly payment × 12.
  • Interest = each month’s balance × the monthly rate (yearly rate ÷ 12), added up for the year.
  • Principal = payments − interest. It is the amount the balance goes down.
  • Balance at end = balance at start − principal. The balance at the end of the last year of the term is the balloon.
  • Each figure is rounded to the nearest dollar for display. Totals are added before rounding, so a total can differ by a dollar or so from adding up the rounded rows.

How the Math Works

Every result on this page comes only from the numbers you type in. The page uses no outside data, market rates or lender rules.

  • Maximum loan by LTV = property value × maximum LTV percent.
  • Yearly payment per $1 of loan = the monthly payment on a $1 loan × 12. The monthly payment on a $1 loan = monthly rate ÷ (1 − (1 + monthly rate) raised to the power of minus the number of months), where the monthly rate is the yearly rate ÷ 12 and the number of months is the amortization years × 12. At 0% interest it is simply 1 ÷ the number of months.
  • Maximum loan by DSCR = (NOI ÷ minimum DSCR) ÷ the yearly payment per $1 of loan. NOI ÷ minimum DSCR is the most the income can pay toward the loan each year.
  • Loan amount = the lower of the two. The lower one is the limit that decides it.
  • Monthly payment = loan amount × the monthly payment on a $1 loan. Yearly payment = monthly payment × 12.
  • DSCR at that loan = NOI ÷ yearly payment.
  • Balloon = the balance still owed after the monthly payments made during the term. It is due in one payment when the term ends, unless you refinance or sell. If the term equals the amortization, the loan is fully paid off and the balloon is $0.

What This Calculator Leaves Out

A simple sizing check. A real loan quote can come out differently.

  • How the lender figures NOI and value. A lender may use its own income, vacancy, expense and value numbers instead of yours.
  • Other loan tests, such as a debt yield, your credit, your other debts, or cash reserves.
  • Loan fees and closing costs, prepayment penalties, and rates that change during the term.
  • Interest-only periods and other payment schedules.
  • Taxes. No tax effects are included.
