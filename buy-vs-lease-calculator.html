How the Math Works

Every result on this page comes only from the numbers you type in. The page uses no outside data, market rates or rules.

  • Leasing, total cash out = the rent for each year plus the NNN costs for each year, added up over the years you compare. Rent starts at your starting rent × square feet and goes up by your rent increase percent each year. Nothing comes back to you at the end of a lease.
  • Buying, total cash out = down payment + closing costs, paid up front, plus every loan payment and every year’s taxes, insurance and operating costs over the years you compare.
  • Monthly loan payment = loan × monthly rate ÷ (1 − (1 + monthly rate) raised to the power of minus the number of months). The loan is the price minus the down payment. The monthly rate is the yearly rate ÷ 12, and the number of months is the amortization years × 12. At 0% interest the payment is simply the loan ÷ the number of months.
  • Estimated value at the end = price × (1 + appreciation percent) for each year compared. For example, 2% for 10 years is price × 1.02 ten times over.
  • Loan balance at the end = what you would still owe after the monthly payments made during the years compared. If the loan is paid off by then, it is $0.
  • Equity built = estimated value at the end − loan balance at the end. It is the same as your down payment + the loan principal you paid off + the change in value.
  • Estimated net sale proceeds = estimated value at the end − selling costs (selling cost percent × that value) − the loan balance you would pay off at the sale.
  • Net cost of buying = buying total cash out − net sale proceeds. Net cost of leasing = leasing total cash out.
  • Net difference = net cost of leasing − net cost of buying. A positive number means buying cost less over the period you chose; a negative number means leasing cost less.

What This Calculator Leaves Out

A simple side-by-side of cash in and cash out. Some real costs and benefits are not in it.

  • Taxes. No income tax, depreciation, interest deduction, or tax on a gain when you sell. These can change the answer a lot. Ask your CPA or tax attorney.
  • The time value of money. A dollar paid in year 10 counts the same as a dollar paid today.
  • What your cash could earn elsewhere. The down payment and closing costs could be invested in your business or somewhere else instead.
  • Costs that change over time. NNN costs and owner costs stay flat. Roof, parking lot and other big repairs are in only if you include them in the owner costs.
  • One-time costs on either side, such as moving, build-out, or a security deposit, unless you add them into the closing costs.
  • Loan details, such as loan fees, a balloon payment due before the loan is paid off, or a rate that changes.
