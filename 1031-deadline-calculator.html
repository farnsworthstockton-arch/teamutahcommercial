How to Identify Replacement Property

From the identification rules in 26 CFR 1.1031(k)-1(c) (read October 21, 2026):

  • Put it in writing and sign it. The property must be designated "in a written document signed by the taxpayer" (c)(2).
  • Deliver it before the end of day 45 to the person who will transfer the replacement property to you (the seller), or to another person involved in the exchange, such as "an intermediary, an escrow agent, and a title company" (c)(2). It can be "hand delivered, mailed, telecopied, or otherwise sent".
  • Not to your own agent. The IRS fact sheet says: "notice to your attorney, real estate agent, accountant or similar persons acting as your agent is not sufficient." (IRS FS-2008-18, February 2008; the IRS now marks it as historical content.)
  • Describe each property clearly, by "a legal description, street address, or distinguishable name (e.g., the Mayfair Apartment Building)" (c)(3).
  • Property you receive before the end of day 45 "will in all events be treated as identified" (c)(1).
  • Changing your mind: an identification "may be revoked at any time before the end of the identification period", in a signed written document sent to the same person you sent the identification to (c)(6).
  • How many: up to three properties of any value (the "3-property rule"), or any number whose total fair market value is not more than 200 percent of the value of what you sold (the "200-percent rule") (c)(4)(i). Identify more than that and you are "treated as if no replacement property had been identified", unless an exception applies, such as receiving, by the exchange deadline, identified property worth at least 95 percent of the value of everything you identified (the "95-percent rule") (c)(4)(ii).

How the Dates Are Counted

When you sell investment or business real estate and use a 1031 exchange to defer the tax, two clocks start on the day the sale closes.

Day 45: name your replacement properties

By the end of day 45 you must name, in writing, the replacement properties you might buy. The regulation says the identification period "ends at midnight on the 45th day thereafter" (26 CFR 1.1031(k)-1(b)(2)(i)).

Exchange deadline: receive the replacement property

You must receive (close on) the replacement property by the end of day 180, or by the due date of your tax return for the year of the sale (including extensions), whichever is earlier. If your return is due before day 180 and you do not extend it, that earlier due date is your real deadline.

"(B) such property is received after the earlier of— (i) the day which is 180 days after the date on which the taxpayer transfers the property relinquished in the exchange, or (ii) the due date (determined with regard to extension) for the transferor’s return of the tax imposed by this chapter for the taxable year in which the transfer of the relinquished property occurs."

26 U.S.C. 1031(a)(3), Cornell LII. Read October 21, 2026; the page shows no currency date.

"The replacement property must be received within 180 days, or by the due date of your tax return (including extensions), whichever is earlier."

IRS, Instructions for Form 8824 (2025). Page Last Reviewed or Updated: 30-Apr-2026. Read October 21, 2026.

Calendar days, counted from the closing date

Day 45 is your closing date plus 45 calendar days. Day 180 is your closing date plus 180 calendar days. This matches the regulation's own worked example:

"M transfers property X to C on November 16, 1992." ... "The identification period ends at midnight on December 31, 1992, the day which is 45 days after the date of transfer of property X." ... "if M is allowed the automatic six-month extension for filing its tax return, the exchange period ends at midnight on May 15, 1993, the day which is 180 days after the date of transfer of property X."

26 CFR 1.1031(k)-1(b)(3), Cornell LII copy of the e-CFR. Read October 21, 2026; the page shows no "current as of" date.

If you sold more than one property in the same exchange on different dates, both periods are counted from the earliest closing date (26 CFR 1.1031(k)-1(b)(2)(iii)).

Weekends and holidays do not move the deadline

The regulation sets each period to end "at midnight" on the 45th or 180th day and has no rule for Saturdays, Sundays or holidays. In its own example above, the 180th day, May 15, 1993, was a Saturday, and that is still the day the exchange period ends. So this calculator never moves a date to the next business day. It warns you instead when a deadline lands on a Saturday, a Sunday or one of the federal legal public holidays listed in 5 U.S.C. 6103(a): New Year’s Day (January 1), Birthday of Martin Luther King, Jr. (third Monday in January), Washington’s Birthday (third Monday in February), Memorial Day (last Monday in May), Juneteenth National Independence Day (June 19), Independence Day (July 4), Labor Day (first Monday in September), Columbus Day (second Monday in October), Veterans Day (November 11), Thanksgiving Day (fourth Thursday in November) and Christmas Day (December 25).

The only IRS extension is for federally declared disasters, and only when the IRS announces relief for that disaster. Rev. Proc. 2018-58, section 17, postpones the last day of the 45-day and 180-day periods in that case, but "in no event may a postponement period extend beyond: (a) the due date (including extensions) of the taxpayer’s tax return for the year of the transfer ... or (b) one year". This calculator does not model disaster relief.

Your tax return due date

Individuals filing a calendar-year return (Form 1040): the IRS "When to file" page shows "Calendar year filers (most common) File on: April 15, 2026" and says you "can request an automatic 6-month extension of time to file." The calculator uses April 15 of the year after your sale for every year, and October 15 of that year if you extend. For a calendar-year individual, day 180 always comes before October 15, so with an extension the deadline is simply day 180.

If April 15 falls on a weekend or holiday, the IRS moves the filing date to the next business day. Ask your CPA whether that changes your exchange deadline. This calculator uses April 15.

Business entities and fiscal-year filers: enter your own return due date, including any extension. Ask your CPA. This calculator does not guess entity due dates.

Real property only

"Under the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, Section 1031 now applies only to exchanges of real property and not to exchanges of personal or intangible property." "However, real property in the United States is not like-kind to real property outside the United States." (IRS, Like-Kind Exchanges - Real Estate Tax Tips.
