Eagle Mountain Commercial Development: One of Utah's Fastest-Growing Cities

Eagle Mountain sits on the western edge of Utah County. It has grown from a quiet rural community into one of the fastest-growing cities in Utah and in the country. This article covers what public sources say about its growth, its road projects and its commercial land.

Population Growth

U.S. Census Bureau estimates put Eagle Mountain at 66,557 residents on July 1, 2025, up from 43,608 in April 2020 and up 8.5% in the latest year alone. By our count from the same Census file, that was the fastest one-year growth of any Utah city with 20,000 or more people, and the third-fastest of any U.S. city with 50,000 or more.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau, city and town population estimates (Vintage 2025)

That growth creates an imbalance commercial investors notice: more than 20,000 new residents since 2020 need goods, services, healthcare, dining, and entertainment close to home. The residential rooftops are already there. The commercial development to serve them is still catching up.

Commercial Development Catching Up to Residential

For years, Eagle Mountain was primarily a bedroom community. Residents commuted east for work, shopping, and medical care. The city's own updates show it working to change that. In March 2025 the city said staff, property owners and developers were working together to recruit healthier grocery stores to Eagle Mountain.

Source: Eagle Mountain City, Rumor Stop

Road Projects

Road access matters more than almost anything else for commercial land in Eagle Mountain. Here is what is funded and scheduled:

  • SR-73 and Mountain View Corridor funding: In May 2024 the Utah Transportation Commission approved $459 million for a Cory Wride Freeway (SR-73) from Mountain View Corridor to Ranches Parkway, and $553 million to extend Mountain View Corridor to SR-73.
  • SR-73 and Mountain View Corridor timing: The city says that work is planned for 2029-2030 and that it is working with UDOT to try to move it up.
  • Pony Express Parkway: The stretch from Eagle Mountain Blvd. to the Public Works building is being widened, expected done fall 2026. There is no timeline yet to widen the whole road to four lanes.
Sources: UDOT, May 20, 2024, Eagle Mountain City, Rumor Stop (September 2026 and April 2026 entries)

Transportation connectivity remains Eagle Mountain's largest infrastructure challenge and opportunity.

What Types of Commercial Property a Growing City Needs

A city that has added more than 20,000 residents since 2020 needs services close to home. Property types that typically follow new rooftops include:

  • Medical office: The growing population needs urgent care, dental, pediatric, and specialty medical services.
  • Grocery-anchored retail: The city says it is working to recruit more grocery stores, and Smith's Marketplace has considered a City Center location.
  • Quick-service and fast-casual restaurants: Pad sites along the main roads serve residents and commuters.
  • Professional office: Insurance, financial services, real estate, and legal offices that serve local residents.
  • Daycare and childcare facilities: Utah has the highest birth rate of any state (13.3 births per 1,000 residents from July 2024 to June 2025, vs. 10.6 nationally), and fast-growing suburbs need licensed childcare space.
  • Self-storage: Rapid population growth and new home construction drive demand for storage facilities.
Source: U.S. Census Bureau, state population estimates (Vintage 2025)

Outlook

Census estimates show the city still growing quickly: 8.5% from July 2024 to July 2025. The biggest road projects are funded, but the city now expects the main SR-73 and Mountain View Corridor work in 2029-2030. For commercial land, that means the population is here now and the major road access is still a few years out.
