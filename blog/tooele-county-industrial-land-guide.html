Industrial land in Tooele County comes with its own set of rules. Who controls the zoning, how water is handled and how the land is taxed can each change what a site is worth to you and how long it takes to build. This guide covers what to check before you sign.

First Question: City or County?

Before anything else, find out which government controls the parcel. Land inside Tooele City follows the city's zoning; land outside any city follows the county's.

  • Tooele City: The city's Planning Division applies city ordinances to subdivisions, commercial site plans and conditional use permits, processes zone changes and variances, and takes development applications through city staff, the Planning Commission and the City Council. Its zoning rules are in Title 7, the Uniform Zoning Title, of the city code, and the city keeps official Zoning and Future Land Use maps (Tooele City Planning Division).
  • Unincorporated Tooele County: The county's Development Department handles long- and short-range planning in unincorporated Tooele County and administers zoning, subdivision and other land use ordinances. Its applications include general plan amendments, rezones, subdivisions and conditional uses (Tooele County Development).

Parcels inside one of the county's other cities follow that city's own code. For the general framework, see our guide to Utah zoning laws for commercial property.

Industrial Zones in Unincorporated Tooele County

Chapter 17 of the Tooele County Land Use Ordinance, listed on the county's website as effective 9-16-26, sets up three industrial-type zones (Tooele County Land Use Ordinance):

Zone Purpose (summarized from the ordinance) Height limit
M-D, Manufacturing Distribution Light manufacturing, industrial processes and warehousing that do not produce objectionable effects 35 feet
M-G, Manufacturing General Heavy industrial processes necessary to the economy No maximum
MG-H, Hazardous Industries Remote locations for storing, treating and disposing of hazardous and radioactive wastes; no new MG-H zones may be created after September 26, 2005 No maximum

Other rules in the same chapter that shape a site plan:

  • In the manufacturing zones, setbacks are set in the conditional use permit or planned unit development approval, but no commercial building may be closer than 50 feet to a residential district boundary line, or to a street line that continues as frontage into a residential district.
  • Buildings and structures may cover no more than 50% of the lot unless a planned unit development approval allows more.
  • The planning commission may require improvements such as street grading and base, curb and gutter, sidewalk, on-site drainage, culinary water facilities, wastewater disposal and street monuments.
  • In M-D, any outdoor area used for anything other than off-street parking and loading must be fully enclosed by a solid fence or wall high enough to screen it from the street and neighbors.

The county's ordinance page also lists a Chapter 17A for commercial tourism, recreation and racing sports, and technology industries zones, and a Chapter 32 Energy Development Zone Overlay listed as effective 11-5-25 (Tooele County Land Use Ordinance). The county notes it does not guarantee the accuracy of the posted documents and suggests calling its office for current copies.

Water: The Make-or-Break Item

Many industrial users need water for process, cooling, fire flow or landscaping, and Tooele Valley is one of the places where new water is hard to get. For the basics of how Utah water rights work, see our due diligence checklist; here is what is specific to Tooele.

State water policy for Tooele and Rush Valleys

The Utah Division of Water Rights policy for Area 15 (Tooele and Rush Valleys), last updated November 3, 2022, says (Division of Water Rights, Area 15 policy):

  • Tooele Valley is closed to new appropriations except for small amounts of shallow ground water (less than 10 feet from the surface) that would otherwise flow to the Great Salt Lake.
  • Surface waters are considered fully appropriated, and new diversions and consumptive uses must be done by change applications on water rights you already own or acquire.
  • A Tooele Valley change application policy adopted April 16, 2008 does not approve changes that move a point of diversion into a designated eastern zone, or more than 3 miles east (measured perpendicular to the existing flow path).
  • Under a July 25, 2019 interim policy, Northern Rush Valley remains open to appropriations of up to 4.73 acre-feet, while the Vernon Area and Southeastern Rush Valley are limited to 1.73 acre-feet for small domestic use or an equivalent small commercial or institutional use.
  • On November 3, 2022, the governor issued a proclamation suspending new appropriations of surplus and unappropriated waters in the Great Salt Lake Basin.

In plain terms: on most Tooele Valley industrial land, plan on buying existing water rights (or getting service from a city or district) rather than filing for new water.

Tooele City's water rights requirement

Inside Tooele City, development comes with a water rights requirement. Draft resolutions in an August 20, 2025 Tooele City Council meeting packet state that Tooele City Code Chapter 7-26 governs the city's exaction of water rights as a condition of land use approval, and that Section 7-26-2(2) lets certain applicants pay the city a fee per acre-foot instead of conveying water rights, under a policy the City Council adopts. The same documents say the council updated that fee-in-lieu policy on November 1, 2023 (Resolution 2023-93), that the council keeps sole discretion over whether to allow the fee, that it weighs factors such as the acre-feet requested, the availability of city-owned water rights, jobs, sales tax, property tax and community impacts, and that the November 1 policy set the fee at $35,000 per acre-foot (Tooele City Council packet, August 20, 2025).

These rules are actively changing. The Tooele City Planning Commission scheduled a public hearing for September 23, 2026 to consider amending Section 7-26-2(3)(a) regarding water rights conveyance requirements and depletion rates for interior uses for new development (Tooele City public notice). Before you price a project, ask the city's Community Development Department for the current requirement and fee.

Where Recent Industrial Projects Have Landed

Tooele City's Projects & News page shows where recent industrial users have chosen to build (Tooele City Economic Development, Projects & News):

  • Peterson Industrial Depot: Central States Manufacturing broke ground on November 2, 2023, and Minova held a groundbreaking on May 13, 2024. Volusol is moving its headquarters from Salt Lake City to a new building there, and the page notes a private rail spur and industrial railroad connections for its manufacturing division.
  • Tooele Boulevard: Leitner-Poma broke ground at 408 South Tooele Boulevard on October 16, 2023 and held its grand opening on June 21, 2024.
  • Earlier Industrial Depot deals: The page also lists Carvana buying land at the Industrial Depot for a westward expansion, and Plastic Ingenuity buying an existing building there with plans to begin operations in 2021.

The city's economic development office lists expanding local jobs and partnering with the county and state to bring in new businesses among its priorities (Tooele City Economic Development).

Property Taxes on Tooele County Land

  • Valuation date: All property is valued as it existed on January 1, valuation notices go out in July or August, real property tax notices are mailed by November 1, and November 30 is the last day to pay without a penalty (Tooele County Assessor, Important Dates).
  • Commercial land values: The county says 2026 values were built from 2025 market information, using a new commercial land guide updated with the latest market information and current zoning (Tooele County, Property Values and Taxes).
  • Appeals: The 2026 appeal deadline was Sept. 15, 2026 for most county parcels and Aug. 1, 2026 for centrally assessed parcels (Tooele County Auditor, Board of Equalization).
  • Business personal property: Equipment and other personal property with a total value per county of $30,100 or less for 2026 qualifies for the exemption (Tooele County Assessor, Personal Property).
  • Greenbelt rollback: If the land is assessed as farmland under the Farmland Assessment Act (greenbelt) and then changes use, a rollback tax covers up to five years of the difference between greenbelt taxes and market-value taxes, and a new owner who wants to keep greenbelt status must file within 120 days of the ownership change (Utah State Tax Commission, Locally Assessed FAQ). Decide in the contract who pays it.

Buyer's Checklist for Tooele Industrial Land

  1. Confirm the jurisdiction (Tooele City, another city, or unincorporated county) and get the current zoning in writing.
  2. Check the use. Read the zone's use table and find out whether your use is permitted or needs a conditional use permit.
  3. Settle the water question early. Identify the water source and any water rights, check the Area 15 policy, and if the site is in Tooele City, ask what Chapter 7-26 will require for your project.
  4. Price the improvements. Roads, curb and gutter, drainage, culinary water and wastewater may be required as conditions of approval.
  5. Verify rail and road access on the ground if your business depends on them, rather than relying on a general description of the area.
  6. Check the tax status, including greenbelt rollback exposure and the January 1 value.
  7. Negotiate enough time. Water change applications and land use approvals can take months, so build that into your due diligence period.
