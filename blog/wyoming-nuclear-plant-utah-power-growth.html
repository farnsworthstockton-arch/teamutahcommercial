Governors Tour Wyoming Nuclear Plant Built to Power Utah Growth

A Construction Site Three States Are Watching

As FOX 13 News first reported, Utah Governor Spencer Cox joined the governors of Idaho and Wyoming this week at a TerraPower construction site in Kemmerer, Wyoming, where Bill Gates's nuclear company is building its Natrium plant. The visit wasn't ceremonial window-dressing. TerraPower has its federal permit and construction plans in hand, and heavy equipment was already clearing ground. Cox called it refreshing to see something that had lived only &quot;on paper&quot; and &quot;in computers&quot; finally go vertical.

For Utah commercial real estate, the headline isn't the reactor pit. It's the power behind it.

Power Capacity Is Becoming a Site Selection Factor

TerraPower expects the plant to produce 500 megawatts at peak, enough for roughly 500,000 homes, and PacifiCorp has lined up to purchase that electricity for its regional grid, which covers Utah along with neighboring states. That matters because the fight for industrial land, data center sites, and heavy manufacturing parcels in Utah increasingly comes down to one question tenants ask before anything else: can this site get enough power, and when?

Data center developers already treat substation capacity the way they once treated freeway access. A three-state governor tour of a reactor designed in part to feed that same regional grid is a signal that power scarcity, not land scarcity, is the next constraint on Utah's industrial growth corridors along the Wasatch Front and in rural counties courting large users.

The Timeline Shapes Today's Lease and Land Decisions

TerraPower's target for first electricity generation is 2031, assuming the project stays on schedule. That is a five-year runway, not an immediate fix. Owners and investors underwriting industrial or data center projects in Utah today should treat this as one input among several, not a near-term power solution. Lease terms, power purchase agreements, and build-to-suit timelines for large energy users in Utah will need to account for a grid that is still years from absorbing this new capacity, which keeps pressure on existing substations and on Utah's parallel small modular reactor experiments in the meantime.

Governor Cox has confirmed he's actively trying to recruit TerraPower into Utah itself, telling FOX 13 that the company's lower water use compared to traditional reactors makes it a better fit for the state. If that recruitment succeeds, it would put an actual generation asset, not just transmission access, inside Utah's borders.

Waste Storage and Community Pushback Aren't Settled

Not everyone on the tour was there to cheer. Utah Senator John Curtis pressed TerraPower executives on spent fuel disposal, and CEO Chris Levesque said the company intends to bury used fuel rather than reprocess it, calling burial the most reliable option. Separately, Lexi Tuddenham of the Healthy Environment Alliance of Utah raised a question that every landowner near a proposed storage site should take seriously: who bears the long-term cost and burden. HEAL Utah is staging a protest in southeastern Utah this weekend over uranium mining tied to nuclear generation, and Tuddenham pointed specifically to Utah leaders' interest in storing waste from other states.

That detail carries real weight for rural Utah land values. Any county positioned as a candidate for waste storage will face a very different investment calculus than one simply sitting near new transmission lines, and that distinction hasn't been resolved publicly.

Gates Hints at More Reactors in the Region

Asked directly whether TerraPower would expand into Utah, Gates declined to rule it out, saying additional reactors will likely be sited &quot;in this area&quot; and that announcements should come within the next year. For owners and tenants tracking Utah's industrial and power-intensive real estate pipeline, that one-year window is worth putting on the calendar. Our team at Team Utah Commercial, reachable at 801-898-8810, fields regular questions from industrial and data center clients about power availability timelines, and this is the kind of regional signal that belongs in those underwriting conversations now, well before the first megawatt flows in 2031.
