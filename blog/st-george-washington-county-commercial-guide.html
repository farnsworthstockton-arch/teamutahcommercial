The St. George and Washington County Commercial Market: A Practical Guide

This guide is for business owners and managers weighing a move into St. George, Washington City or elsewhere in Washington County. It skips the forecasts and covers the practical side: which government you will deal with, what the official numbers say, how water affects commercial projects here, and the steps the cities publish for licensing and development.

If you want our take on the national demand ranking that put St. George in the headlines, see St. George Tops NAR's New National CRE Demand Index. This guide does not repeat it. Every figure below links to the government or university page we read it on, and we checked each source on October 21, 2026.

First Question: Which City Is Your Site In?

People say "St. George" for the whole area, but the city line matters. Licensing, zoning, sign rules and development review are handled by the city your site is in, or by the county if the site is outside every city.

  • Washington County says its Community Development Office issues business licenses for businesses located within the unincorporated areas of the county, and that businesses operating within an incorporated city or town should contact the appropriate municipal office (Washington County, Business License Information; read October 21, 2026).
  • The county's cities and towns include St. George, Washington, Hurricane, Ivins and Santa Clara, plus several smaller cities and towns, in the Census Bureau's population estimates (sources below).

Before you tour, confirm which jurisdiction the parcel is in. St. George publishes a GIS Interactive Map, and each city's planning office can confirm it for you.

Growth, in Official Numbers

The U.S. Census Bureau's Vintage 2025 estimates give resident population on July 1, 2025, compared with the April 1, 2020 estimates base:

  • Washington County: 213,670, up from 180,284
  • St. George: 108,713, up from 95,348
  • Washington City: 37,107, up from 28,000
  • Hurricane: 25,745, up from 20,042
  • Ivins: 11,546, up from 8,977
  • Santa Clara: 8,722, up from 7,560

Source: Census Bureau, City and Town Population Totals: 2020-2025, Utah data file sub-est2025_49.csv; read October 21, 2026.

What this means for you: growth brings customers and workers, and it also brings construction, new competitors and change around any site. Ask what is planned near a building, not just what is there today. St. George publishes a "Coming Soon to St. George" map, and Washington City lists current and planned projects on its website.

Jobs and Wages

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics' County Employment and Wages release for the first quarter of 2026 shows, for Washington County:

  • 8.4 thousand business establishments in the first quarter of 2026
  • 91.7 thousand jobs in March 2026, up 1.9 percent from March 2025. BLS said Washington County had the largest over-the-year increase in employment among Utah's five largest counties.
  • An average weekly wage of $1,042 in the first quarter of 2026, up 7.0 percent from a year earlier. The statewide Utah figure in the same table was $1,415.

Source: BLS, County Employment and Wages in Utah, First Quarter 2026, released September 4, 2026; data are preliminary; read October 21, 2026.

What this means for you: jobs here grew faster than in Utah's other large counties over that year, and wages rose quickly, but the average weekly wage was still below the statewide figure. If your business depends on hiring, build local wage levels into your plan.

Water Belongs on Your Checklist From Day One

In Washington County, water deserves attention early in any commercial decision. Two official points stand out.

One-time water impact fees

The Washington County Water Conservancy District says its impact fees are one-time payments to fund the construction of public facilities needed to serve new development. The district says fees are due prior to securing a building permit and can't be transferred to another lot once paid. Its standard single-meter fees for non-residential connections, as listed on October 21, 2026:

  • 3/4″ or 5/8″ meter: $19,856
  • 1″ meter: $53,524
  • 1.5″ meter: $131,219
  • 2″ meter: $328,049

The district adds that non-standard fees are calculated individually, and that fees may differ based on the size of the irrigated area and other factors (Washington County Water Conservancy District, Impact Fees; read October 21, 2026).

In plain English: if your business will need a new water connection or a bigger meter, the one-time water fee can change the math on a site. Ask the district and the city which fees apply to your exact plan before you commit to a space.

Landscaping rules for new commercial projects

A Utah State University Extension case study written by two Washington County Water Conservancy District managers says non-functional turfgrass has been banned for new commercial, institutional, and industrial developments in Washington County. It also describes the Virgin River basin, the region's primary water source, as fully allocated (USU Extension, Case Study: Water Efficiency Ordinances in Southwest Utah; no publication date shown; read October 21, 2026). If you are building or expanding, plan for water-wise landscaping from the start, and confirm the current landscaping rules with the city your site is in.

Working With the City of St. George

The City of St. George describes itself as located along Interstate 15, near the Las Vegas market, and says St. George Regional Airport is the largest regional airport in the State of Utah (City of St. George, Economic Development; read October 21, 2026). Here is how its published process works:

  • Zoning. The city's zoning rules are its Title 10 Zoning Code. The Planning and Zoning page says all development within the City of St. George starts there (City of St. George, Planning and Zoning).
  • Early meetings with city staff. St. George holds Development Staff Review (DSR) and Pre-Application (PAM) meetings each Tuesday between 8:30 am and 11:00 am, in person at the city's Development Conference Room at 61 S Main Street. A DSR meeting must come before a Pre-Application meeting and requires a conceptual plan uploaded ahead of time; the city says DSR meetings are meant to help the public understand the required steps for a potential project, with various city departments represented. A Pre-Application meeting reviews a site plan and utility drawings. Requests are first come, first served and are taken until 12:00 pm on the Friday before your selected date (City of St. George, DSR and Pre-Application Meetings).
  • Applications. The city routes plats and site plans through its CityInspect portal, and planning applications and sign permits through its Citizen Serve portal. The Planning Commission meets on the second and fourth Tuesdays of each month starting at 5 pm, unless otherwise noticed (City of St. George, Planning and Zoning; read October 21, 2026).
  • Business licenses. St. George takes business license applications and renewals through its CitizenServe portal, points to Title 3 of the city code for business licensing rules, and publishes a General Business License Checklist (City of St. George, Business Licensing).

In plain English: if your space needs real changes, such as new site work, a new building or new utility connections, a DSR meeting is an early, structured way to hear from several city departments at once, before you are locked into a lease or purchase.

Working With Washington City

Washington City's economic development page features three areas: the Sunrise Valley Industrial Park, the ARA Southwest Logistics Center and the Interstate 15 Buena Vista Commercial Corridor. The city also publishes a Public Infrastructure District (PID) policy and an online PID application (Washington City, Economic Development; read October 21, 2026).

Its business license page lays out the steps that come before the city license (Washington City, Doing Business in Washington City; read October 21, 2026):

  • Register your business name with the Utah Department of Commerce, apply for a federal ID number, and get a Utah State Sales Tax Identification Number if you sell taxable goods. The city asks for copies of these with the business license application, which has its own Commercial & Industrial version.
  • Planning and Zoning approval is required for all signage.
  • Some businesses need other approvals: Southwest Utah Public Health Department approval for food establishments and some other uses; City of St. George Regional Water Reclamation Facility approval for food service establishments, waste haulers, car washes, commercial truck washes, auto repair, and industrial facilities discharging any wastewater; and stormwater approval for activities that may impact the storm drain system, such as mobile washing, concrete pumping or grooming.

In plain English: get your state registrations done first, and if your business uses water in its work or serves food, expect more than one approval before you open.

A Washington County Site Checklist

  1. Confirm which city (or the county) has jurisdiction over the parcel.
  2. Confirm that the zoning allows your exact use, in writing if the city offers it.
  3. Ask whether your use needs a new or larger water meter, and what the one-time impact fees would be.
  4. If your business discharges wastewater or could affect storm drains, ask which approvals apply.
  5. If you are building or expanding, plan for water-wise landscaping and confirm the city's current rules.
  6. In St. George, book a DSR meeting early if your project involves site work or a new building.
  7. Check the sign rules before you design your signs.
  8. Finish your state registrations, then apply for the city business license.
  9. Ask what is planned near the site, and have your attorney review the lease or purchase agreement before you sign.
