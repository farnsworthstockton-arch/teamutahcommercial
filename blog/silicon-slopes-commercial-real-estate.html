Silicon Slopes Effect: How Utah's Tech Boom Is Reshaping Commercial Real Estate

Utah has a growing technology sector. Centered along the stretch of I-15 from Draper to Lehi and extending into South Jordan, Sandy, and American Fork, the area branded as Silicon Slopes drives commercial real estate demand across many property types in the northern Utah County and southern Salt Lake County corridors.

What Is Silicon Slopes?

Silicon Slopes is both a geographic designation and a nonprofit organization promoting Utah's tech industry. Geographically, the core tech corridor runs along the I-15 and Bangerter Highway corridors through Lehi, Draper, South Jordan, Sandy, and American Fork. This stretch is home to a concentration of technology companies.

The corridor's Utah County side has grown fast. U.S. Census Bureau county population estimates (Vintage 2025) show Utah County, home to Lehi and American Fork, growing from 659,392 people in April 2020 to 759,859 on July 1, 2025, up 15.2%. The concentration of tech talent has created a self-reinforcing cycle: companies locate here to access the workforce, which attracts more workers, which draws more companies.

Major Tech Employers Driving Demand

The Silicon Slopes corridor is home to a diverse mix of established technology companies, high-growth scale-ups, and early-stage startups. Major employers with significant office footprints in the corridor span enterprise software, cybersecurity, financial technology, health tech, and e-commerce sectors.

These companies are not just consuming office space. Their employees need housing, retail services, restaurants, fitness facilities, childcare centers, and healthcare providers, all of which generate secondary demand for commercial real estate throughout the corridor.

Tech employment in the corridor affects more than offices. It also touches retail, industrial, housing, and flex space.

Office Market Transformation

Office space along the Silicon Slopes corridor now includes more than traditional long-term, full-building leases:

  • Class A campus-style offices with amenities like fitness centers, cafeterias, outdoor workspaces, and EV charging stations
  • Flexible lease terms that accommodate the unpredictable growth trajectories of tech companies
  • Build-to-suit projects for larger tenants who need custom floor plates and infrastructure
  • Spec office development targeting the steady stream of companies looking for move-in-ready space

Tech tenants typically look for modern building systems, robust internet infrastructure, and thoughtful workspace design.

Ripple Effects on Retail, Industrial, and Housing

Retail

Tech workers add to retail demand along the corridor. Communities like Lehi's Traverse Mountain, South Jordan's Daybreak, and Draper's commercial nodes have retail that serves them: specialty restaurants, boutique fitness studios, grocery stores, and family-oriented entertainment venues.

Industrial and Data Centers

Tech sector growth also adds demand for industrial space, such as data centers and last-mile distribution facilities.

Housing Pressure

Job and population growth put pressure on housing throughout northern Utah County and southern Salt Lake County (Utah County alone grew 15.2% from 2020 to 2025, as noted above). This housing demand, in turn, creates opportunities for multifamily developers and investors, particularly in the growing transit-oriented development zones along UTA's FrontRunner and TRAX lines.

Venture Capital and Startup Culture

Venture capital is part of the Silicon Slopes picture. The corridor has attracted investment across a broad range of sectors including fintech, cybersecurity, healthcare IT, and B2B software.

This venture activity matters for commercial real estate because funded startups are immediate consumers of office and flex space. A company that closes a Series A round typically needs to expand its physical footprint within months, creating a steady pipeline of demand for small-to-medium office suites in the corridor.

The Silicon Slopes organization plays an important role in fostering this ecosystem through events, mentorship programs, and community building.

Co-Working and Flex Space Growth

Co-working operators, executive suite providers, and flex office concepts in the Silicon Slopes corridor serve several parts of the tech market:

  • Early-stage startups that need professional space without long-term lease commitments
  • Remote workers employed by out-of-state tech companies who want local community and meeting space
  • Enterprise satellite teams that need small Utah offices for regional sales, support, or engineering groups
  • Scale-ups in transition that have outgrown their current space but have not yet committed to a long-term lease

Flex space demand can create opportunities for building owners to reposition underperforming properties. Converting traditional office space into managed flex environments can increase effective rents and reduce vacancy risk by diversifying the tenant base across multiple small occupiers rather than relying on a single large tenant.

Emerging Tech Submarkets

While Lehi and Draper remain the core of Silicon Slopes, several adjacent areas are worth watching as the corridor extends:

  • American Fork and Pleasant Grove: Lehi's neighbors along I-15, next to the core of the corridor.
  • Vineyard and Orem: The Utah Valley University area and the Vineyard development along Utah Lake.
  • Point of the Mountain: The Point is 600 acres of state-owned land in Draper, the former Utah State Prison site, which the state is developing as what it calls "Utah's Innovation Community."
  • Herriman and Riverton: Residential growth in these communities brings demand for follow-on commercial development.

What This Means for CRE Investors

The Silicon Slopes effect presents several actionable opportunities for commercial real estate investors:

  • Office repositioning: Older office properties in the corridor can be renovated to meet tech tenant expectations.
  • Retail development: Following rooftops and employment centers in the tech corridor can support retail investment.
  • Flex/industrial plays: Small-bay industrial and flex properties near the corridor serve both tech companies and the service businesses that support them.
  • Land banking: Undeveloped parcels in emerging submarkets like Vineyard, Point of the Mountain, and the Mountain View Corridor could benefit if the corridor keeps expanding.
