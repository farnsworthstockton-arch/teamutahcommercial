Intermountain Breaks Ground on 80,000 SF Ogden Medical Building

Market Spotlight October 23, 2026 Team Utah Commercial 4 min read

A New Building for an Old Problem

Intermountain Health has broken ground on an 80,000-square-foot medical office building at McKay-Dee Hospital in Ogden, as first reported by DOTmed. The facility is part of the hospital's North Campus expansion and is expected to open in spring 2028, consolidating specialty clinics including sleep medicine, diabetes care, endocrinology and pediatrics under one roof.

The move addresses a familiar constraint for growing health systems: clinical space scattered across a main hospital campus eventually runs out of room to expand. Several clinics now operating in McKay-Dee's existing medical office building will relocate to the new structure, which frees up square footage on the primary campus for other uses. That's a pattern worth watching closely if you own or lease adjacent commercial property, because hospital systems rarely let freed space sit empty for long.

Outpatient Demand Keeps Driving the Build-Out

This is not a standalone project. Intermountain describes it as the latest phase of a North Campus expansion that already includes a recently opened endoscopy center and the Porter Family Medicine Clinic, alongside a surgery center and sports medicine clinic that have operated there for years. Judy Williamson, president of McKay-Dee Hospital, called the project "an important investment in the health of our growing community."

The through-line across all of these additions is outpatient care moving out of inpatient hospital space and into purpose-built clinic buildings. That's been the dominant trend in healthcare real estate nationally for over a decade, but Weber County's version of it is accelerating. Four distinct outpatient facilities built or planned on one campus inside a few years tells you Intermountain expects sustained population growth along the Wasatch Front's northern corridor, not just a temporary capacity crunch.

What It Signals for Land Near Hospital Campuses

Intermountain didn't disclose construction costs or projected patient volume, which limits how precisely investors can size the opportunity. But the shape of the project tells its own story. Medical office buildings tied to a hospital system carry long-term lease stability that's hard to find in other asset classes, and they tend to pull ancillary demand toward them: pharmacies, imaging centers, urgent care, even medical retail looking for proximity to a captive patient base.

Owners of land and existing buildings within a short drive of North Campus should expect that pull to intensify as the 2028 opening approaches. Weber County hasn't historically commanded the same investor attention as Salt Lake or Utah County, but a hospital system committing to its fourth phase of expansion on one campus is the kind of signal that tends to pull capital north over time.

The Space Left Behind

The more interesting question for brokers may not be the new building at all, but what happens to the clinic space being vacated inside McKay-Dee's existing medical office building. Hospital systems generally don't let freed-up clinical square footage go unused for long, whether that means backfilling with a different specialty, administrative functions, or in some cases releasing it for lease to outside providers. Anyone tracking medical office vacancy and absorption in Weber County should watch for announcements tied to that existing building well before the new one opens.
