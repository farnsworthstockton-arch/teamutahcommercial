Industrial Outdoor Storage (IOS) in Utah: What It Is, Who Needs It, What to Look For

Some businesses need a warehouse. Others mainly need ground: a fenced, drivable yard to park trucks and trailers, stage equipment, or store materials, with maybe a small office or shop on the side. That second kind of property is called industrial outdoor storage, or IOS. This guide explains what to look for before you lease or buy one in Utah.

For the wider Utah industrial picture, see our Utah industrial market report. This post sticks to the practical checks for a yard.

What Is Industrial Outdoor Storage?

IOS describes industrial property with a large outdoor component, used for storage, heavy industrial uses, or truck and vehicle parking. Its value depends more on the location and the improved land than on the buildings. Common traits include:

  • Low building coverage: under 20% of the site.
  • Non-specialized improvements, rather than a purpose-built building.
  • Location near logistics or transportation infrastructure.
  • Size range: infill sites of 0.5 to 3 acres; larger "mega" sites of 50+ acres in outlying areas.
Source: Colliers Knowledge Leader, "The Inside Scoop on Outdoor Storage: The Rapid Rise of IOS" (August 15, 2024), read October 21, 2026.

Who Needs an IOS Yard

Typical IOS users include logistics companies, local landscaping and building supply companies, and utility and school bus companies. Federal lending rules point to a few more: the SBA's lending manual gives outdoor storage yards for general contractors, trucking companies, and moving and storage companies as examples of outdoor space actively used in a business. If your business parks a fleet or keeps equipment or materials outside, the checks below apply to you too.

Sources: Colliers Knowledge Leader (August 15, 2024); U.S. Small Business Administration, SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), Occupancy and Leasing Requirements. Both read October 21, 2026.

If you plan to buy a yard for your own business, that SBA detail matters: an actively used storage yard can count toward the space your business occupies under SBA loan rules (parking areas do not). Our guide to buying your building with an SBA 504 loan explains the occupancy rules.

Zoning: Check This First

The Colliers report calls zoning the largest factor in a successful IOS site: the best sites allow a variety of outdoor storage uses by right, meaning no special approval is needed. The same report notes that cities are increasingly hesitant to approve new IOS uses, which makes good sites harder to entitle.

Two recent Utah examples show why you should never assume:

  • Salt Lake City started a zoning text amendment (petition PLNPCM2024-01412) to clarify the definitions of "Storage (Outdoor)" and "Storage, Public (Outdoor)" and to evaluate which zones should permit commercial outdoor storage of vehicles and equipment. The city held an online open house on August 12, 2025. We could not confirm whether the change has been adopted, so ask the city's planning division for the current rules.
  • West Jordan handled a request for outdoor storage in its M-1 (light industrial) zone through an administrative conditional use permit. The approval came with conditions, including one that "A 20-foot emergency access driveway shall be maintained around the building at all times."
Sources: Colliers Knowledge Leader (August 15, 2024); Utah Public Notice Website, Salt Lake City Planning Division open house notice (posted July 28, 2025); West Jordan Zoning Administrative Hearing minutes (dated and approved February 9, 2026). All read October 21, 2026.

What to do: get the zone name, then read the city's land use table to see whether outdoor storage is permitted, conditional, or not allowed, and what extra standards apply. Ask the city in writing about your specific use (truck parking, equipment storage, materials storage and repair work can be treated differently). Our Utah zoning guide explains permitted vs. conditional uses, and the CRE Glossary defines terms like setback, overlay district and variance.

Surfacing, Dust and Stormwater

The ground is the product, so look hard at it. Ask whether the yard is paved, recycled asphalt, or compacted gravel; whether it can carry loaded trucks without rutting; and where the water goes when it rains or the snow melts. Then check three Utah rules:

  • Dust control. Utah's Division of Air Quality says any person owning or operating a source of fugitive dust within PM10 and PM2.5 non-attainment and maintenance plan areas on cleared land greater than 1/4 acre in size must submit a Fugitive Dust Control Plan (Utah Administrative Code R307-309-6). Ask DAQ whether your site is in one of those areas. Even a small infill IOS site (0.5 acre and up, per the Colliers range above) is over that quarter-acre line.
  • Construction stormwater. If grading or resurfacing will disturb one or more acres, Utah's Division of Water Quality points you to the UPDES General Permit for Construction Activities.
  • Industrial stormwater. Some uses need coverage under the state's Multi-Sector Industrial Storm Water Permit, based on the business's SIC code. Examples on the state's list: motor freight transportation facilities (SIC 40-43, 5171, 4221-4225), where the vehicle maintenance areas (repairs, painting, fueling, lubrication) and equipment cleaning areas are what count; automobile salvage yards (SIC 5015); and scrap recycling (SIC 5093).
Sources: Utah DEQ, Air Quality Fees and Payments (fugitive dust); Utah DEQ, Storm Water Permit Guide; Utah DEQ, SIC Codes Permitting Requirements. All read October 21, 2026.

If the previous user fueled, washed or repaired vehicles on the site, plan on an environmental review before you buy or sign a long lease. Our Utah due diligence checklist walks through Phase I environmental assessments.

Truck Access and State Highways

A yard is only as good as its gate. Walk the site with the biggest vehicle you run in mind: Can a truck and trailer turn in without crossing the center line? Is there room to stack trucks inside the gate instead of on the street? Are there height or weight limits on nearby roads or bridges?

If the site fronts a state highway, the Utah Department of Transportation (UDOT) has its own say. UDOT requires a Conditional Access Permit along a state highway "Whenever a driveway or local street connection is requested," "When there is a change in land use (e.g. change in zoning, site plan, or conditional use)," and "When there is a change in intensity of an existing access." Turning a quiet lot into a busy truck yard can fit the last one. UDOT's process starts with a pre-application meeting with the UDOT Region office, then an online application and a non-refundable review fee.

Source: UDOT, Conditional Access Permit (Access Management), read October 21, 2026.

Fencing, Screening and Security

A yard full of trucks or equipment needs a secure perimeter, and cities can also regulate how outdoor storage is screened from streets and neighbors. Rules differ from city to city, so confirm the required fence height, material (solid wall, slatted chain-link, or other) and gate location before you budget. In the West Jordan approval above, the yard was to be enclosed by chain-link fencing with privacy slats and solid metal gates, and the conditions set exactly where the gates had to sit.

Also ask about lighting, cameras, gate controls, and whether the city or fire department needs emergency access through the gate.

Source: West Jordan Zoning Administrative Hearing minutes (February 9, 2026), read October 21, 2026.

Utilities and the Small Building

The building covers only a small share of an IOS site, but it still matters. Ask about:

  • Power: enough capacity for the shop, yard lighting, gate motors, and any block heaters or chargers. Colliers notes that developers are increasingly running conduit for electric delivery van charging at new IOS sites.
  • Water and sewer: restrooms for drivers and staff, and any wash bay (which brings its own drainage rules).
  • Internet: for dispatch, cameras and gate access.
  • The building itself: office, shop bays, overhead door sizes, and whether it has a certificate of occupancy for your use.
Source: Colliers Knowledge Leader (August 15, 2024), read October 21, 2026.

Leasing a yard? Find out whether the lease passes the property taxes, insurance and upkeep to you. Utah taxes commercial property, land included, on 100 percent of its fair market value, so a tax pass-through on a multi-acre yard can be real money. Our guide to gross vs. NNN vs. modified gross leases shows how to compare quotes. Also get in writing what condition the surface must be in when you leave.

Source: Utah State Tax Commission, 2024 Property Tax Annual Statistical Report (August 2025), read October 21, 2026.

IOS Site Checklist

Topic Ask or check
Zoning Is your exact use permitted by right? If conditional, what conditions are likely? Any pending code changes?
Surfacing Paved, recycled asphalt or gravel? Will it hold loaded trucks? Where does water drain?
Dust and stormwater Is a Fugitive Dust Control Plan needed? Will grading disturb one acre or more? Does your SIC code need industrial stormwater coverage?
Access Truck turning room, stacking space inside the gate, road limits. On a state highway, is a UDOT Conditional Access Permit needed?
Fencing and security Required screening, fence height and material, gate location, lighting, cameras, emergency access.
Utilities Power capacity, water and sewer, internet, wash bay drainage.
Environmental Past fueling, repair or washing on site? Plan a Phase I before you commit.
Lease terms Who pays taxes, insurance and upkeep? What surface condition is required at move-out?
