Overview

This article looks at what government sources show about the forces behind industrial demand in Utah: jobs, property values, the labor market and population growth. Vacancy, rents and cap rates come from paid market research, so we send those by request (see below).

Utah's position along the I-15 and I-80 corridors, combined with access to rail infrastructure and the Salt Lake City International Airport, makes the state a natural distribution point for companies serving the Intermountain West.

Industrial Jobs

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics counts Utah jobs by industry every month. September 2026 figures (preliminary, seasonally adjusted) compared with September 2025:

  • Manufacturing: 155,900 jobs, up 1,900 (1.2%) from 154,000.
  • Trade, transportation and utilities: 315,200 jobs, up 4,000 (1.3%) from 311,200.
  • Transportation and warehousing (part of the line above): 78,600 jobs, up 2,700 (3.6%) from 75,900.
  • All nonfarm jobs: 1,797,000, up 26,300 (1.5%) from 1,770,700.

Source: BLS Current Employment Statistics, Utah: manufacturing, trade, transportation and utilities, transportation and warehousing, total nonfarm.

Manufacturing and warehousing jobs are the ones that fill industrial buildings, so gains in those lines are worth watching for anyone who owns or leases industrial space.

Commercial & Industrial Property Value

The taxable value of locally assessed commercial and industrial land and buildings in Utah was $132.0 billion in 2025, up 7.93% from 2024. Utah taxes commercial property at its full market value as estimated by each county assessor. The figure combines commercial and industrial property and includes hotels and resorts.

Source: Utah State Tax Commission, Property Tax Division, 2025 Annual Statistical Report, Table 1A, “Commercial & Industrial” column. County-by-county figures are on our Utah market heatmap.

Unemployment

Utah's seasonally adjusted unemployment rate was 3.5% in September 2026 (preliminary), compared with 4.2% nationally. A low rate means a tight labor market, which matters for warehouse and manufacturing users that plan to hire.

Sources: BLS, Utah unemployment rate and U.S. unemployment rate.

Population Growth

Utah grew by 35,921 residents (1.0%) between July 1, 2024 and July 1, 2025, to 3,538,904. Since 2020 Utah has grown 8.2%, fifth-fastest among the states, vs. 3.1% for the U.S. More people means more goods to store and deliver.

Source: U.S. Census Bureau, state population estimates (Vintage 2025).
