Davis County and Layton Industrial and Flex Space: What Businesses Should Know

If your business needs a warehouse, a shop with a roll-up door, or a flex unit with an office up front and work space in back, Davis County and Layton are probably on your list. This guide is for the owner or manager doing that search. It covers what the official numbers say about the area, how Hill Air Force Base and the highways shape where industrial space sits, and the city rules that most often surprise a business after it has already picked a building.

Every figure below links to the government page we read it on, and we checked each source on October 21, 2026. Rules and numbers change, so confirm anything important with the city or county before you sign.

Industrial or Flex: Know What You Are Shopping For

Listings use the words "industrial" and "flex" loosely, so start by being precise about what your business needs.

  • Industrial space is built for making, storing or moving goods. What usually matters most is loading (dock-high doors or ground-level roll-up doors), clear height (the usable height under the roof structure), electrical power, truck access and yard space.
  • Flex space mixes finished space, such as an office, showroom or lab, with shop or warehouse space in the same unit. Contractors, service companies, light assemblers, online sellers and trades often fit here.

The label on a listing does not decide what you are allowed to do in a building. The city's zoning does. That is why the zoning section below matters more than any brochure. If a lease term is new to you, our CRE Glossary has plain-English definitions.

Davis County and Layton by the Numbers

A few official numbers give useful context for a business deciding where to locate:

  • Population. The U.S. Census Bureau estimates Davis County had 381,227 residents on July 1, 2025, up from an April 1, 2020 estimates base of 362,676. Layton, the county's largest city in the same estimates, had 85,308 residents on July 1, 2025, up from 81,828. (Source: Census Bureau, City and Town Population Totals: 2020-2025, Utah data file sub-est2025_49.csv; read October 21, 2026.)
  • Businesses, jobs and wages. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics counted 10.4 thousand business establishments in Davis County in the first quarter of 2026, and 141.5 thousand jobs in March 2026, a change of -1.3 percent from March 2025. The county's average weekly wage in the first quarter of 2026 was $1,257. BLS marks these figures as preliminary. (Source: BLS, County Employment and Wages in Utah, First Quarter 2026, released September 4, 2026; read October 21, 2026.)
  • Land. Davis County's Community and Economic Development office says the county has "over 3,000 acres of developable land" and that it works with its 15 cities to create shovel-ready sites. (Source: Davis County Economic Development; read October 21, 2026.)

What this means for you: Davis County keeps adding residents, which means more nearby customers and more people available to hire. The same BLS snapshot shows the county's job count slipped a little over that year, so do not assume every part of the local economy is moving in the same direction. And these are countywide numbers. The building you lease will succeed or fail on its own access, zoning and condition.

Hill Air Force Base and Layton's Business Parks

Layton City's economic development office calls Hill Air Force Base a substantial contributor of economic activity for the city, and says the base's main South Gate and its East Gate are both in Layton. The city reports that in 2024 the base created approximately $2.1 billion in annual federal payroll and $12.7 billion in total annual economic impact, and employed 26,893 personnel (Layton City, Hill Air Force Base; read October 21, 2026).

For a business looking at space, the base matters in three practical ways:

  1. Defense and aerospace suppliers are part of the neighborhood. Layton lists aerospace and composites among its key industries and describes a large private defense research, development and manufacturing industry next to the base (Layton City, Key Industries). The city's maps page offers brochures for two named business parks, the East Gate Business Park and the West Layton Business Park, plus East Gate Business Park design guidelines and a master plan (Layton City, Maps & Information). If your site is inside a business park, read its design guidelines as well as the zoning. They are a separate document.
  2. Land near the base has its own maps. Layton's planning and zoning maps include an "AICUZ/APZ Map" (the Air Force's compatible-use and accident potential zones around the base), and the city links the Hill Air Force Base Compatible Use Plan, which it says was completed in 2022 (Layton City, Maps & Information; Layton City, Hill Air Force Base; read October 21, 2026). If your site is anywhere near the base, look at these maps before you sign and ask the city how they affect your planned use.
  3. Redevelopment areas shape what gets built nearby. Layton's Redevelopment Agency manages three redevelopment areas: the East Gate Economic Development Area, the South Main/South Fort Lane Redevelopment Area and the Antelope Drive Community Development Area. In these areas, the city explains, the increase in property taxes created by new development can be returned to the investor or developer, typically for costs like public infrastructure (Layton City, Incentives). This matters most to owners and developers, but it can change what gets built next to your business.

The Roads That Shape the Map

For a distribution, trade or service business, the path from your door to the freeway matters as much as the freeway itself.

  • Davis County lists Interstate 15, Interstate 84, Highway 89 and the West Davis Corridor as its main arterial routes, and points to its connection to Salt Lake International Airport (Davis County Economic Development).
  • The Utah Department of Transportation describes SR-177 (also known as West Davis Highway) as a 16-mile highway that currently runs from Farmington to West Point, and says it is working on a project to extend the highway 2.5 miles north to 1800 North in West Point (UDOT, SR-177 (West Davis); read October 21, 2026).
  • Layton publishes a Truck Routes map on its Maps & Information page. If trucks will serve your site, check the route between the site and the highway, and drive it at the time of day your deliveries will actually happen.

Zoning in Layton: The Rule That Trips Up Flex Users

Layton's zoning rules are in Title 19 of the city's municipal code. The city's Planning & Zoning page links straight to the "Land Use Categories by Zone" section (19.06.000) and the "Lot Area & Setbacks" section (19.05.000). Layton has more than one manufacturing zone; city documents refer, for example, to the M-1 zoning district and to the M-2 (Heavy Manufacturing/Industrial) zoning district (Layton City Council packet, September 18, 2025).

The 40% limit on commercial uses in manufacturing zones

This is the rule we most want flex tenants to know about. A Layton City Council work meeting packet for September 18, 2025 quotes the city code's limit on commercial uses in manufacturing zoning districts (section 19.06.170): no more than 40% of the total interior building area of a development site may be occupied by uses listed in the city's Table 6-2 as "L4." City staff wrote that the purpose of the limit is to allow commercial and retail uses on a small scale while keeping most of the industrial area for industrial and manufacturing uses (Layton City Council work meeting agenda and packet, September 18, 2025, posted on the Utah Public Notice website; read October 21, 2026).

In plain English: if you want to put a gym, studio, showroom or other commercial use into a building in a Layton manufacturing zone, the answer can depend on what the other tenants in the same development already do. A space can look perfect and still not qualify for a business license because the development's commercial share is already used up.

The same packet described a proposed change, Ordinance 25-21, that would create an exemption to the limit for athletic uses in the M-2 zone, for buildings whose footprint sits wholly within 275 feet of an arterial road right-of-way. We could not confirm from public sources whether the City Council adopted it, so as of October 21, 2026, treat that change as unconfirmed and ask the city (September 18, 2025 packet).

Get the zone in writing

Layton offers an online Zoning Verification Letter Request. The city says the request "is intended to provide a verification of the zone of a property and general information regarding allowing land uses" (Layton City, Zoning Verification; read October 21, 2026). Ask for one, describe your actual use in detail, and do it before you sign a lease or a letter of intent.

Business Licenses and Permits Before You Move In

  • Layton says a valid Layton City business license is required for all businesses located within the Layton City incorporated limits. New applications go through the city's CityInspect portal (Layton City, Business Licensing; read October 21, 2026).
  • Each Layton business license expires 1 year from the date of issuance. If your business has a location change, name change or ownership change, the city says the current license is not transferable and a new business license application must be submitted. So a move from one Layton building to another still means a new application (Layton City, Business Licensing).
  • The licensing page lists a Commercial Property Permission Form and a Fire and Life Safety Checklist among its resources. Download both early so nothing surprises you close to move-in.
  • Building and sign permits go through Layton's online portal. The city says it moved to a fully digital building and sign permit application portal as of September 5, 2019 (Layton City, Building & Sign Permits). If your space needs new walls, electrical work or signs, build permit time into your move date.
  • If your site is in another Davis County city, that city runs its own zoning, licensing and permits. Ask that city's planning office the same questions.

A Davis County Site Checklist

  1. Write down your real use: what you will make, store, sell or fix, how many people will be on site, and whether customers will visit.
  2. Get the zone confirmed in writing. In Layton, use the Zoning Verification Letter Request.
  3. In a manufacturing zone, ask how much of the development's commercial ("L4") allowance is already in use.
  4. If the site is near Hill Air Force Base, check the AICUZ/APZ map.
  5. Check the truck route from the site to the highway.
  6. Confirm the building basics in person: power, loading doors, clear height, fire sprinklers, parking and yard.
  7. Find out what build-out and permits your use needs before you agree on rent and dates.
  8. Start the business license application early. In Layton, a move means a new license application.
  9. Have your attorney review the lease before you sign.
