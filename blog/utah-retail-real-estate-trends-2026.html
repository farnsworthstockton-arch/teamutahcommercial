Utah Retail Real Estate in 2026: What Population and Sales Data Show

This article looks at what government data shows about retail demand in Utah: how fast the population is growing, how many babies are being born, and how much is being spent at retailers. Vacancy and rents come from paid market research, so we send those by request (see below).

Population Growth

Utah grew by 35,921 residents (1.0%) between July 1, 2024 and July 1, 2025, to 3,538,904, according to U.S. Census Bureau Vintage 2025 estimates. Since 2020 Utah has grown 8.2%, fifth-fastest among the states, vs. 3.1% for the U.S. New households need goods and services close to where they live.

The Highest Birth Rate in the Nation

Utah had the highest birth rate of any state from July 2024 to June 2025: 13.3 births per 1,000 residents, vs. 10.6 nationally. Births outnumbered deaths by 24,961 that year (U.S. Census Bureau). Young families drive demand for groceries, childcare, pediatric and dental offices, and family dining.

Taxable Retail Sales

Utah State Tax Commission figures show retailers reported $56.43 billion in taxable sales and purchases in calendar 2025, up 3.79% from $54.37 billion in 2024. That total combines every retail category in the state's report, from car dealers to online sellers. Some categories:

  • Nonstore retailers (mostly online sellers): $9.23 billion, up 9.36%, the fastest growth of any retail category.
  • General merchandise stores: $10.65 billion, up 4.87%.
  • Food and beverage stores: $7.12 billion, up 2.10%.
  • Restaurants and bars (counted separately from retail): $8.54 billion, up 4.96%.

Online sellers grew fastest, but store-based retail still grew: taxable sales at stores (every retail category except nonstore retailers) rose 2.76%, from $45.93 billion to $47.20 billion. These figures are not adjusted for inflation. Source: Utah State Tax Commission, 2025 annual taxable sales report, Table 2.
