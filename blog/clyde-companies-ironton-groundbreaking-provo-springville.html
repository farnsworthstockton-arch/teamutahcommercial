Clyde Companies Breaks Ground on 31-Acre Ironton District in Provo

Market Spotlight November 12, 2026 Team Utah Commercial 4 min read

Table of Contents

  2. Two Cities, One Site Plan
  3. Phase One and the Headquarters Next Door
  4. An Industrial Name on a New Use

A Centennial Groundbreaking

Clyde Companies broke ground November 9 on Ironton, a 31-acre mixed-use district straddling the Provo-Springville border, as first reported by Utah Business. The timing isn't incidental. The privately held company traces its roots to a 1926 road-building outfit founded in Springville, and Ironton lands in the firm's centennial year, a hundred years after Wilford W. Clyde started with a single crew in the same city.

CEO Jeremy Hafen called it part of the company's mission to invest "right here where it all began." For a firm with seven subsidiaries and roughly $3 billion in annual revenue, that's not a rounding-error project — it's a deliberate statement about where Clyde wants its name attached going forward.

Two Cities, One Site Plan

Ironton sits at 1400 North and Main Street along U.S. 89, and the site plan splits uses right along the municipal line. Provo gets the housing — 168 townhomes and 90 condo units. Springville gets the commercial load — more than 67,000 square feet of retail and 27,526 square feet of office space. That's an unusual arrangement for a single project, and it required Provo and Springville to coordinate infrastructure and land-use decisions across a boundary that, as Mayor Marsha Judkins put it, won't matter to "the people who will live and spend time here."

For brokers working both sides of that line, this is worth tracking closely. A 258-unit housing push on the Provo side adds real rooftops within walking distance of a retail base that didn't exist a year ago. Retail and office tenants eyeing Springville now have a built-in residential customer base forming next door, rather than having to build demand from scratch.

Phase One and the Headquarters Next Door

The first phase — 60,000 square feet of retail and office space, an ice-skating ribbon, and a central event lawn — is targeted for completion in 2028. That's one year behind Clyde's own move: the company's new 125,000-square-foot mass-timber headquarters, under construction directly south on Main Street as part of its "Springville Rising" initiative, is expected to be operational in 2027.

Running the two timelines in parallel is a calculated bet. Clyde will have a few hundred employees working across the street from Ironton before its own retail and office space is finished, which gives the project a built-in daytime tenant base before it ever markets to outside retailers. Owners and tenants evaluating space along that stretch of U.S. 89 should expect leasing interest to concentrate around that 2027-2028 window rather than spread evenly over the next several years.

An Industrial Name on a New Use

The project's name nods to Columbia Steel Corporation's pig iron plant, which operated on the site between Provo and Springville from 1924 until U.S. Steel closed it in the early 1960s — once among the largest pig iron operations in the western U.S. Converting that kind of industrial legacy site into a walkable retail-and-residential district is a pattern CRE investors have seen play out elsewhere in Utah County, and it tends to reset land values for everything adjacent once the first phase delivers.

Clyde Companies hasn't set a completion date for Ironton's later phases, which is the detail worth watching most. First-phase delivery in 2028 gives the market a clean benchmark, but owners of nearby parcels along Main Street and investors sizing up retail pad sites should ask Clyde or the cities directly about sequencing before assuming a timeline. Our team at Team Utah Commercial (801-898-8810) is tracking lease-up activity on both the Provo and Springville sides as space comes to market, since early absorption numbers here will say a lot about how fast this corridor fills in.
