What the three lease types mean

Short, plain-English versions. Each one links to the full entry in our CRE Glossary.

Gross lease

You pay one rent. The landlord pays the building's property taxes, insurance and CAM out of that rent. It is easy to budget for, but the rent is usually higher because it has to cover those costs.

A full service lease is a close cousin: the landlord usually covers even more, such as utilities.

Gross lease in the glossary
Full service lease in the glossary

NNN (triple net) lease

You pay base rent plus your share of the three "nets": property taxes, insurance and CAM. The rent number looks lower, but those extra costs are on you, and they can change from year to year.

NNN lease in the glossary

Modified gross lease

A middle ground. The landlord pays some of the costs and you pay the rest. Who pays what is negotiated, so read the lease closely.

This page splits costs by type: you tick which ones the landlord pays. Some modified gross leases split costs another way, such as a base year expense stop, which this page does not model.
